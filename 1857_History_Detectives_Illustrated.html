Case file 01

The Chain Reaction

Rebuild the early sequence and explain how a military mutiny grew into a wider rebellion.

EVIDENCE FILE

Illustrative artwork for learning — these are not original photographs or eyewitness images.

Clue A · 10 May 1857Sepoys in Meerut broke out in mutiny. The action spread from the infantry to the cavalry and then to the town.
Clue B · That nightGovernment buildings were attacked, and the telegraph line to Delhi was cut. A group of sepoys rode towards Delhi.
Clue C · 11 May 1857Sepoys reached the Red Fort and sought Bahadur Shah's blessing. His association gave the revolt a form of legitimacy.

Investigator's question

Which explanation best connects the clues?

Put the events in date order. Ask yourself what changed when the sepoys reached Delhi and sought the Mughal emperor's blessing.
Points: up to 15
Case file 02

The Rumours That Would Not Die

Investigate why rumours and prophecies mattered during a period of fear and mistrust.

EVIDENCE FILE

Illustrative artwork for learning — these are not original photographs or eyewitness images.

Clue A · CartridgesSepoys feared that the new cartridges were greased with cow and pig fat, offending the religious beliefs of Hindus and Muslims.
Clue B · FlourA rumour spread that the British had mixed the bone dust of cows and pigs into flour sold in the market.
Clue C · ProphecyA prophecy said that British rule would end one hundred years after the Battle of Plassey (1757). The centenary fell in 1857.

Investigator's question

What is the strongest conclusion supported by these clues?

A historian asks two different questions: “Did people believe this?” and “Was the claim proven true?” Do not confuse them.
Points: up to 15
Case file 03

Why Awadh Rose

Connect political change, landholding, and the links between soldiers and village life.

EVIDENCE FILE

Illustrative artwork for learning — these are not original photographs or eyewitness images.

Clue A · 1856The British annexed Awadh and exiled Nawab Wajid Ali Shah.
Clue B · LandThe chapter says the Summary Settlement of 1856 reduced the share of villages held by taluqdars from 67% to 38%.
Clue C · Village linksMany Bengal Army sepoys came from Awadh and eastern Uttar Pradesh. News and concerns moved between sepoy lines and their villages.

Investigator's question

Bring together all three clues. A strong answer should explain several connected causes without claiming that every group wanted the same thing.

Case file 05

Whose History Is It?

Investigate how pictures can present events from a particular point of view.

EVIDENCE FILE

Illustrative artwork for learning — these are not original photographs or eyewitness images.

Clue A · British paintingRelief of Lucknow (1859) places British leaders at the centre and presents the relief of the British garrison as a triumph.
Clue B · Press imagesThe chapter explains that some British images portrayed rebels as cruel and encouraged public anger and demands for revenge.
Clue C · Nationalist memoryLater nationalist images presented leaders such as Rani Lakshmi Bai as heroic figures resisting foreign rule.

Investigator's question

Which statement best explains what historians can learn from these images?

The chapter says images did more than reflect feelings: they also helped shape them. Compare what each image asks its audience to feel.
Points: up to 15

The Detective's Final Verdict

FINAL REPORT

Your task

Question: Why should the Revolt of 1857 be understood through several causes, social groups, and historical perspectives rather than through one simple explanation?

Write 5–8 sentences. Use details from the case files. You may mention the sequence from Meerut to Delhi, rumours, Awadh, rebel and British records, or visual representations.

How your verdict is scored (25 points):
• Uses evidence from at least three cases (10 points)
• Explains connections between causes or groups (5 points)
• Recognises limits or perspectives in historical sources (5 points)
• Gives a clear conclusion in your own words (5 points)
Note: This score is a self-check, not an automatic judgement of your history knowledge. Your teacher can review the reasoning.

Teacher's note

Suggested use: 35–45 minutes, individually or in teams of 3–4. The multiple-choice answers check core understanding; the final verdict checks whether students can connect evidence and explain their reasoning. Discuss the final written verdict with students rather than relying on the score alone.

Content accuracy: The game focuses on the chapter's themes and examples. It does not claim that all regions or social groups had identical experiences or aims. Rumours are presented as beliefs reported in the chapter, not as proven facts.
